## Why this section exists

CenCal Health's role is easy to misunderstand because several different systems operate at once. A prescribing decision, a coverage decision, a utilization-management decision, a pharmacy claim, and a state or federal program requirement are related, but they are not the same legal decision.

This section maps those layers so a provider can ask the right question of the right entity.

## A critical distinction: prescribing is not coverage

A health plan does not become the prescriber merely because it reviews a request for authorization. Conversely, a prescription does not automatically establish that the plan must pay for the requested treatment.

The analysis should therefore remain separate:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Clinical indication">What does the treating clinician believe is medically appropriate for this patient, and why?</Step>
  <Step title="Legal prescribing authority">What California and federal prescribing rules govern the clinician's decision?</Step>
  <Step title="Benefit coverage">Is the requested service or drug a covered benefit under the member's applicable program and contract?</Step>
  <Step title="Utilization management">Does the benefit require prior authorization, step therapy, a TAR, or another utilization-management process?</Step>
  <Step title="Medical necessity determination">If authorization is required, what legally applicable criteria and evidence does the plan use to determine medical necessity?</Step>
  <Step title="Notice and appeal">If the request is denied, modified, or delayed, what explanation, criteria, grievance, appeal, or external-review pathway applies?</Step>
</Steps>

## CenCal's documented role

CenCal Health publicly describes itself as a Medi-Cal managed care plan serving Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo Counties. Its provider materials identify utilization management, authorizations, pharmacy services, provider responsibilities, and medical-necessity criteria as plan functions.

For physician-administered drugs, CenCal describes a Medical Pharmacy Management program that uses written objective criteria based on sound clinical evidence. Its published criteria sources include FDA labeling, pivotal trials, CMS-approved compendia, evidence-based guidelines, peer-reviewed literature, and other identified sources. When criteria are used as the basis for a decision to modify, delay, or deny a service, CenCal states that the criteria are disclosed to the member and provider in that specific case.

<Callout intent="info">
This distinction is central to an off-label request. The provider's clinical rationale and the plan's coverage or authorization determination should be documented as separate propositions, with each tied to its governing authority.
</Callout>

## Pharmacy is a separate benefit pathway

CenCal states that, since January 1, 2022, the Medi-Cal Rx pharmacy benefit is administered by DHCS rather than by CenCal Health. That means a pharmacy-dispensed outpatient prescription may follow a different authorization and claims pathway from a physician-administered drug billed through CenCal's medical benefit.

<Callout intent="warning">
Before assigning a pharmacy issue to CenCal Health, identify whether the request concerns the Medi-Cal Rx pharmacy benefit, a CenCal medical-benefit physician-administered drug, or another benefit/program. The responsible entity and applicable criteria can differ.
</Callout>

## What a provider should be able to request or receive

A useful authorization process should make the operative criteria and decision understandable. CenCal's published materials state that its specific medical-pharmacy criteria are available to providers upon request and that authorization submissions should include supporting documentation for medical justification.

For an adverse determination, the record should be evaluated for:

- the requested service or drug;
- the benefit under which it was requested;
- the clinical criteria actually applied;
- the factual information considered;
- the reason for denial, modification, or deferral;
- the clinical rationale supporting the determination;
- the applicable appeal or grievance route; and
- any external-review pathway available under the governing program.

## Governing authority hierarchy

The site will treat these sources separately rather than collapsing them into one category called “CenCal policy”:

1. Federal Medicaid statutes, regulations, and CMS-approved requirements.
2. California statutes and regulations.
3. DHCS Medi-Cal managed-care contracts and applicable DHCS guidance.
4. CenCal Health plan policies and provider materials.
5. Clinical criteria and evidence used in a particular utilization decision.
6. The individual authorization, denial, grievance, appeal, or external-review record.

<Callout intent="info">
A plan's internal policy can explain how the plan operates, but it does not by itself establish what California or federal law requires. Conversely, a statute or regulation may impose a duty that is not accurately captured by a simplified provider-facing policy. The registry therefore preserves the source and authority level for every proposition.
</Callout>

## Accountability and review

California's Department of Managed Health Care regulates health care service plans subject to its jurisdiction under the Knox-Keene Act. Medi-Cal managed care is also subject to DHCS oversight and contract requirements. The applicable regulator and review pathway therefore depend on the plan, benefit, and issue presented.

For a Medi-Cal managed-care question, the site should identify the DHCS contract and applicable Medi-Cal requirements before assuming that the ordinary commercial-plan pathway applies.

## Research note: CenCal-specific findings

A DHCS survey of CenCal's Specialized Plan for Developmental Services identified deficiencies involving denial notices that did not consistently provide a clear explanation, the criteria or guidelines used, and the clinical reasons for the decision, as well as timing and notification problems for certain pharmaceutical prior authorizations. Those findings are historical and program-specific; they should be treated as evidence for the site's accountability research corpus, not generalized automatically to current CenCal operations.

This section will eventually connect individual findings, corrective actions, policies, and later decisions to the same authority and decision data model used elsewhere in the site.
