**Journal:** The Lancet Psychiatry (2026)

**Authors:** Mikail Nourredine, Florent Subtil, Samuele Cortese

**Medication:** Amphetamines, Methylphenidate, Guanfacine, Atomoxetine

**Usage Type:** Off-label (unlicensed high-dose) and Dose Titration Support

**Study Type:** Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis

**Country:** Global (UK, France, International data)

**DOI:** [10.1016/S2215-0366(26)00091-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(26)00091-X)

## Key Findings

Identified dose-effect patterns across age groups. For amphetamines, efficacy plateaued at ~25mg/day in children and ~50mg/day in adults. Methylphenidate showed no clear plateau in children but peaked at ~50mg/day in adults. Found that escalation beyond licensed limits may improve efficacy modestly but increases adverse event risk.

## Argument Support

The study provides a comprehensive dose-effect network meta-analysis, including doses beyond FDA-recommended maxima. It supports upward titration from minimum doses when symptoms remain insufficiently controlled and explores the benefits vs. risks of unlicensed (high) doses.
