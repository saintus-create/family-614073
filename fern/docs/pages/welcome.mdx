## How the system stays coherent

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Prescribing">**Clinical decision:** Is the proposed use medically indicated for this patient, and what California requirements govern the prescription?</Tab>
  <Tab title="Evidence">**Evidence dossier:** What supports efficacy and safety, what limits confidence, and what material evidence points the other way?</Tab>
  <Tab title="Coverage">**Authorization:** What separate payer, public-program, or workers' compensation rules determine coverage or reimbursement?</Tab>
  <Tab title="Plan accountability">**Plan role:** Which entity administers the benefit, which authority governs it, what criteria were actually applied, and what review or appeal route follows an adverse determination?</Tab>
</Tabs>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Primary authority comes first">Statutes and regulations are distinguished from agency guidance, clinical guidelines, and peer-reviewed evidence. The application should never silently convert an advisory source into a mandatory requirement.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Off-label does not mean unlawful">The workflow treats off-label status as a classification that triggers evidence and documentation questions, not as an automatic prohibition or permission.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Prescribing and coverage are different decisions">The treating clinician makes the clinical prescribing decision. A plan or program may separately determine whether a requested service is a covered benefit and whether authorization requirements are satisfied.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Approved and denied claims are evidence about decisions">Real-world authorization outcomes will be catalogued with their fact pattern, evidence, cited authority, decision rationale, and appeal history. They will not be presented as substitutes for the governing law.</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
