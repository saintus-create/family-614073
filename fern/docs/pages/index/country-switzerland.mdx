## Evidence synthesis

#### [Subcortical brain volume differences in participants with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder in children and adults: a cross-sectional mega-analysis](/pmid-28219628)

Hoogman M, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2017 Apr. PMID: 28219628 · Meta-analysis

With the largest dataset to date, we add new knowledge about bilateral amygdala, accumbens, and hippocampus reductions in ADHD. We extend the brain maturation delay theory for ADHD to include subcortical structures and refute medication effects on brain volume suggested by earlier meta-analyses. Lifespan analyses suggest that, in the absence of well powered longitudinal studies, the ENIGMA cross-sectional sample across six decades of ages provides a means to generate hypotheses about lifespan trajectories in brain phenotypes.

#### [Pharmacological interventions for ADHD: a systematic review and dose-effect network meta-analysis](/pmid-42134365)

Nourredine M, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2026 Jun. PMID: 42134365 · Systematic review

Our findings challenge both therapeutic inertia-accepting suboptimal response without further dose titration-and uncritical dose escalation beyond licensed limits, when potential harms outweigh expected benefits. Our findings can inform clinical guidelines and should support shared decision making regarding ADHD medication dosage.

#### [Comparative cardiovascular safety of medications for attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder in children, adolescents, and adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis](/pmid-40203844)

Farhat LC, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2025 May. PMID: 40203844 · Systematic review

Practitioners should monitor blood pressure and pulse in patients with ADHD treated with any pharmacological intervention, and not stimulants only. Given the short duration of available randomised controlled trials, new research providing insights on the causal effects of ADHD medications on cardiovascular parameters in the longer term should be funded.

#### [Comparative efficacy and acceptability of pharmacological, psychological, and neurostimulatory interventions for ADHD in adults: a systematic review and component network meta-analysis](/pmid-39701638)

Ostinelli EG, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2025 Jan. PMID: 39701638 · Systematic review

Stimulants and atomoxetine were the only interventions with evidence of beneficial effects in terms of reducing ADHD core symptoms in the short term, supported by both self-reported and clinician-reported ratings. However, atomoxetine was less acceptable than placebo. Medications for ADHD were not efficacious on additional relevant outcomes, such as quality of life, and evidence in the longer term is underinvestigated. The effects of non-pharmacological strategies were inconsistent across different raters. Our network meta-analysis represents the most comprehensive synthesis of available evidence to inform future guidelines in the field.

#### [Efficacy and safety of established and off-label ADHD drug therapies for cognitive impairment or attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder symptoms in bipolar disorder: A systematic review by the ISBD Targeting Cognition Task Force](/pmid-38433530)

Miskowiak KW, et al. Bipolar Disord. 2024 May. PMID: 38433530 · Systematic review

Methylphenidate or mixed amphetamine salts may improve ADHD symptoms in BD. However, there is limited evidence regarding the effectiveness on cognition. The medications produced no increased mania risk when used alongside mood stabilizers. Further robust studies are needed to assess cognition in BD patients receiving psychostimulant treatment alongside mood stabilizers.

#### [Comparative efficacy and tolerability of medications for attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder in children, adolescents, and adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis](/pmid-30097390)

Cortese S, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2018 Sep. PMID: 30097390 · Systematic review

Our findings represent the most comprehensive available evidence base to inform patients, families, clinicians, guideline developers, and policymakers on the choice of ADHD medications across age groups. Taking into account both efficacy and safety, evidence from this meta-analysis supports methylphenidate in children and adolescents, and amphetamines in adults, as preferred first-choice medications for the short-term treatment of ADHD. New research should be funded urgently to assess long-term effects of these drugs.

## Observational studies

#### [Does attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder medication reduce entrepreneurship?](/pmid-32567214)

Peltonen J, et al. Health Econ. 2020 Sep. PMID: 32567214 · Cross-sectional study

In particular, person-level studies suggest that ADHD-like behavior may possess some advantages for engaging in entrepreneurship and the initiation of new businesses, which is considered a key activity for economic development. Using recent panel data from 11 countries and one special administrative region (SAR), we investigate if the increasing use of ADHD medication in adults is associated with an unintended outcome of reducing entrepreneurship.
