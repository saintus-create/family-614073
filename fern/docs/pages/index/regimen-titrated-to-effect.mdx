### [Evaluation of Methylphenidate Safety and Maximum-Dose Titration Rationale in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Meta-analysis](/pmid-31135892)

Ching C, Eslick GD, Poulton AS

JAMA Pediatr. 2019 Jul;173(7):630-639. doi: 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2019.0905. PMID: 31135892 · Meta-analysis

A range of maximum doses for methylphenidate was recommended in clinical studies; no discernable scientific justification for any particular dose was given. Reports of life-threatening adverse events were absent; further studies of the efficacy, tolerability, and safety of methylphenidate titrated purely on clinical grounds, without reference to any set maximum dose, are needed.

### [Pharmacological interventions for ADHD: a systematic review and dose-effect network meta-analysis](/pmid-42134365)

Lancet Psychiatry. 2026 Jun;13(6):485-495. doi: 10.1016/S2215-0366(26)00091-X. PMID: 42134365 · Systematic review

Our findings challenge both therapeutic inertia-accepting suboptimal response without further dose titration-and uncritical dose escalation beyond licensed limits, when potential harms outweigh expected benefits. Our findings can inform clinical guidelines and should support shared decision making regarding ADHD medication dosage.

### [A double-blind, placebo-controlled study of atomoxetine in young children with ADHD](/pmid-21422081)

Kratochvil CJ, Vaughan BS, Stoner JA, Daughton JM, Lubberstedt BD, Murray DW, Chrisman AK, Faircloth MA, et al. (15 authors)

Pediatrics. 2011 Apr;127(4):e862-8. doi: 10.1542/peds.2010-0825. PMID: 21422081 · Randomized controlled trial

To our knowledge, this is the first randomized controlled trial of atomoxetine in children as young as 5 years. Atomoxetine generally was well tolerated and reduced core ADHD symptoms in the children on the basis of parent and teacher reports. Reductions in the ADHD-IV Rating Scale scores, however, did not necessarily translate to overall clinical and functional improvement, as demonstrated on the Clinical Global Impression-Severity Scale and the Clinical Global Impression-Improvement Scale. Despite benefits, the children in the atomoxetine group remained, on average, significantly impaired at the end of the study.
