### [Prenatal Exposure to Acid-Suppressive Medications and Risk of Neuropsychiatric Disorders in Children](/pmid-41499135)

Hong S, Lee S, Kim H, Jo H, Lee K, Jo Y, Kim TH, Park J, et al. (12 authors)

JAMA. 2026 Feb;335(7):609-618. doi: 10.1001/jama.2025.23956. PMID: 41499135 · Cohort study

Exposure to acid-suppressive medication during pregnancy was not associated with children's risk of ADHD, severe neuropsychiatric disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, intellectual disability, or ASD in sibling-control analyses. Small associations were observed in overlap-weighted models; these may reflect confounding by shared familial factors.

### [Combined Methylphenidate and Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors in Adults With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder](/pmid-39382893)

Lee DY, Kim C, Shin Y, Park RW

JAMA Netw Open. 2024 Oct;7(10):e2438398. doi: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.38398. PMID: 39382893 · Cohort study

Results of this study revealed no significant increase in adverse event risk associated with use of SSRI plus methylphenidate vs methylphenidate alone in adults with ADHD and comorbid depression. Instead, the combination was associated with a lower risk of headache.

### [Global burden of ADHD medication-associated cardiovascular disease, 1967-2023: A comparative analysis using the WHO pharmacovigilance database](/pmid-39241651)

Cho H, Lee K, Jeong YD, Udeh R, Acharya KP, Kang J, Boyer L, Fond G, et al. (14 authors)

Asian J Psychiatr. 2024 Nov;101:104209. doi: 10.1016/j.ajp.2024.104209. PMID: 39241651

The associations between CVDs and ADHD medications vary, with amphetamines posing a higher risk, while lisdexamfetamine and methylphenidate exhibit better safety profiles.
