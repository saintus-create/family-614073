## Evidence synthesis

#### [ADHD and adherence to antihypertensive medication treatment: a multinational cohort study](/pmid-41721349)

Yao H, et al. BMC Med. 2026 Feb. PMID: 41721349 · Meta-analysis

Adults with ADHD are more likely to discontinue antihypertensive treatment and exhibit poor medication adherence. However, ADHD medication use appears to be associated with better adherence among individuals with ADHD.

#### [Psychiatric and neurological predictors of early ADHD medication discontinuation across the lifespan: a multinational study](/pmid-42392681)

Brikell I, et al. BMJ Ment Health. 2026 Jul. PMID: 42392681 · Meta-analysis

Children with neuropsychiatric comorbidity and related comedication are more likely to discontinue ADHD medication early, whereas few consistent predictors were seen from adolescence onwards. Marked cross-country variation, particularly in the USA, points to system-level influences on treatment patterns.

#### [Comparative efficacy and tolerability of medications for attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder in children, adolescents, and adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis](/pmid-30097390)

Cortese S, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2018 Sep. PMID: 30097390 · Systematic review

Our findings represent the most comprehensive available evidence base to inform patients, families, clinicians, guideline developers, and policymakers on the choice of ADHD medications across age groups. Taking into account both efficacy and safety, evidence from this meta-analysis supports methylphenidate in children and adolescents, and amphetamines in adults, as preferred first-choice medications for the short-term treatment of ADHD. New research should be funded urgently to assess long-term effects of these drugs.

## Trials

#### [Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial on the Efficacy, Safety and Tolerability of Modified-Release Methylphenidate (MPH-MR) in Chinese Children and Adolescents with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)](/pmid-39671144)

Zheng Y, et al. CNS Drugs. 2025 Mar. PMID: 39671144 · Randomized controlled trial

Modified-release methylphenidate was superior to placebo in improving ADHD symptoms and functioning in Chinese pediatric patients with ADHD. Modified-release methylphenidate had a good safety and tolerability profile. Trial Registration http://www.chinadrugtrials.org.cn/ Identifier: CTR20180056 (registered on 21 May 2018).

#### [The promoting effects of digital targeted cognitive training in medication treatment for children with ADHD: a randomized controlled trial](/pmid-40598377)

Dang C, et al. BMC Med. 2025 Jul. PMID: 40598377 · Randomized controlled trial

These findings demonstrated that, in comparison to medication monotherapy, the TCT + M treatment did not lead to more improvements in the core symptoms of ADHD, nor did it show superiority in other secondary outcomes. Specifically in children treated with atomoxetine, there's a potential promoting effect of targeted cognitive training on medication treatment in terms of the alleviation of ADHD core symptoms.

#### [A Digital Cognitive-Physical Intervention for Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: Randomized Controlled Trial](/pmid-38728075)

Zhao L, et al. J Med Internet Res. 2024 May. PMID: 38728075 · Randomized controlled trial

This novel digital cognitive-physical intervention was efficacious in school-age children with ADHD. A larger multicenter effectiveness trial with longer follow-up is warranted to confirm these findings and to assess the durability of treatment effects.

#### [Long-term safety of methylphenidate in children and adolescents with ADHD: 2-year outcomes of the Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder Drugs Use Chronic Effects (ADDUCE) study](/pmid-36958362)

Man KKC, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2023 May. PMID: 36958362 · Randomized controlled trial

Our results suggest that long-term treatment with methylphenidate for 2 years is safe. There was no evidence to support the hypothesis that methylphenidate treatment leads to reductions in growth. Methylphenidate-related pulse and blood pressure changes, although relatively small, require regular monitoring.

## Pharmacokinetics

#### [The Effect of Myricetin on Pharmacokinetics of Atomoxetine and its Metabolite 4-Hydroxyatomoxetine In Vivo and In Vitro](/pmid-27207674)

Lan T, et al. Eur J Drug Metab Pharmacokinet. 2017 Apr. PMID: 27207674 · Pharmacokinetic study

Our study showed that myricetin could significantly inhibit the formation of atomoxetine metabolite both in vivo and in vitro. It is recommended that the effect of myricetin on the metabolism of atomoxetine should be noted and atomoxetine plasma concentration should be monitored.

## Other records

#### [Comparison of serious adverse effects of methylphenidate, atomoxetine and amphetamine in the treatment of ADHD: an adverse event analysis based on the FAERS database](/pmid-39980035)

Wu L, et al. BMC Pharmacol Toxicol. 2025 Feb. PMID: 39980035

In this study, pharmacovigilance analysis of methylphenidate, atomoxetine, and amphetamine was performed using the FAERS database, and we identified significant safety signals. Of note, three ADHD medications are associated with suicide-related signals, amphetamine associated with coronary artery dissection, methylphenidate associated with precocious puberty, and atomoxetine associated with testicle, penile lesions, and liver damage, which require special attention. This study provides a reference for the clinical personalized medication of ADHD patients.

#### [ADHD medication discontinuation and persistence across the lifespan: a retrospective observational study using population-based databases](/pmid-38035876)

Brikell I, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2024 Jan. PMID: 38035876

Early medication discontinuation is prevalent in ADHD treatment, particularly among young adults. Although reinitiation of medication is common, treatment persistence in adolescents and young adults is lower than expected based on previous estimates of ADHD symptom persistence in these age groups. This study highlights the scope of medication treatment discontinuation and persistence in ADHD across the lifespan and provides new knowledge about long-term ADHD medication use.
