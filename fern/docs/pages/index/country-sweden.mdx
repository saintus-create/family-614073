## Evidence synthesis

#### [ADHD and adherence to antihypertensive medication treatment: a multinational cohort study](/pmid-41721349)

Yao H, et al. BMC Med. 2026 Feb. PMID: 41721349 · Meta-analysis

Adults with ADHD are more likely to discontinue antihypertensive treatment and exhibit poor medication adherence. However, ADHD medication use appears to be associated with better adherence among individuals with ADHD.

#### [Psychiatric and neurological predictors of early ADHD medication discontinuation across the lifespan: a multinational study](/pmid-42392681)

Brikell I, et al. BMJ Ment Health. 2026 Jul. PMID: 42392681 · Meta-analysis

Children with neuropsychiatric comorbidity and related comedication are more likely to discontinue ADHD medication early, whereas few consistent predictors were seen from adolescence onwards. Marked cross-country variation, particularly in the USA, points to system-level influences on treatment patterns.

#### [Methylphenidate for children and adolescents with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)](/pmid-41342306)

Storebø OJ, et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2025 Dec. PMID: 41342306 · Meta-analysis

Our updated meta-analyses suggest that methylphenidate versus placebo or no-intervention may improve teacher-rated ADHD symptoms and general behaviour in children and adolescents with ADHD. There may be no effects on serious adverse events and quality of life.

#### [Risk of Cardiovascular Diseases Associated With Medications Used in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis](/pmid-36416824)

Zhang L, et al. JAMA Netw Open. 2022 Nov. PMID: 36416824 · Meta-analysis

This meta-analysis suggests no statistically significant association between ADHD medications and the risk of CVD across age groups, although a modest risk increase could not be ruled out, especially for the risk of cardiac arrest or tachyarrhythmias. Further investigation is warranted for the cardiovascular risk in female patients and patients with preexisting CVD as well as long-term risks associated with ADHD medication use.

#### [Binge-Eating Disorder in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis](/pmid-27367316)

Brownley KA, et al. Ann Intern Med. 2016 Sep. PMID: 27367316 · Meta-analysis

Cognitive behavioral therapy, lisdexamfetamine, SGAs, and topiramate reduced binge eating and related psychopathology, and lisdexamfetamine and topiramate reduced weight in adults with binge-eating disorder.

#### [Benefits and harms of ADHD interventions: umbrella review and platform for shared decision making](/pmid-41297970)

Gosling CJ, et al. BMJ. 2025 Nov. PMID: 41297970 · Systematic review

This review provides updated evidence to inform patients, practitioners, and guideline developers how best to manage ADHD symptoms. The online platform should facilitate the implementation of shared decision making in daily practice.

#### [Comparative cardiovascular safety of medications for attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder in children, adolescents, and adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis](/pmid-40203844)

Farhat LC, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2025 May. PMID: 40203844 · Systematic review

Practitioners should monitor blood pressure and pulse in patients with ADHD treated with any pharmacological intervention, and not stimulants only. Given the short duration of available randomised controlled trials, new research providing insights on the causal effects of ADHD medications on cardiovascular parameters in the longer term should be funded.

## Trials

#### [Evaluating ADHD medication trial representativeness: a Swedish population-based study comparing hypothetically trial-eligible and trial-ineligible individuals](/pmid-39788146)

Garcia-Argibay M, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2025 Feb. PMID: 39788146 · Randomized controlled trial

Individuals ineligible for ADHD medication trials face higher risks of adverse outcomes. This study provides the first empirical evidence for the limited generalisability of ADHD RCTs to real-world clinical populations, by applying eligibility criteria extracted from a comprehensive dataset of RCTs to a large real-world cohort. Triangulating evidence from RCTs and real-world studies is crucial to inform rigorous evidence-based treatment guidelines.

#### [The Effect of Methylphenidate on Cognition in Patients with Comorbid Attention Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and Amphetamine Use Disorder: An Exploratory Single-Blinded within-Subject Study](/pmid-38029734)

Brynte C, et al. Eur Addict Res. 2024. PMID: 38029734 · Randomized controlled trial

Patients with ADHD+AMPH present with more severe neurocognitive deficits compared to ADHD only. The effect of 180 mg OROS-MPH on cognition in patients with ADHD+AMPH was inconclusive. Future studies should consider recruitment issues and high drop-out rates in this study population.

#### [Effects of one single-dose methylphenidate compared to one single-dose placebo on QbTest performance in adults with untreated ADHD: a randomized controlled trial](/pmid-37848887)

Jansson L, et al. BMC Psychiatry. 2023 Oct. PMID: 37848887 · Randomized controlled trial

There seems to be an immediate placebo response in the core symptom inattention. The effect of placebo cannot be ruled out and must be taken in consideration during drug trials with continuous performance tests (CPTs).

#### [Randomised controlled trial of the short-term effects of osmotic-release oral system methylphenidate on symptoms and behavioural outcomes in young male prisoners with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: CIAO-II study](/pmid-35657651)

Asherson PJ, et al. Br J Psychiatry. 2023 Jan. PMID: 35657651 · Randomized controlled trial

ADHD symptoms did not respond to OROS-methylphenidate in young adult prisoners. The findings do not support routine treatment with OROS-methylphenidate in this population. Further research is needed to evaluate effects of higher average dosing and adherence to treatment, multi-modal treatments and preventative interventions in the community.

#### [Computerized training of working memory in children with ADHD--a randomized, controlled trial](/pmid-15689731)

Klingberg T, et al. J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry. 2005 Feb. PMID: 15689731 · Randomized controlled trial

This study shows that WM can be improved by training in children with ADHD. This training also improved response inhibition and reasoning and resulted in a reduction of the parent-rated inattentive symptoms of ADHD.

## Observational studies

#### [Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Medication Use in Pregnancy and Risk of Preterm Birth: A Population-Based Cohort Study](/pmid-40364699)

Srinivas C, et al. Paediatr Perinat Epidemiol. 2026 Feb. PMID: 40364699 · Cohort study

ADHD medication may modestly increase the risk of preterm birth, especially with atomoxetine early and methylphenidate late in pregnancy, and with longer durations of use.

#### [Methylphenidate and Short-Term Cardiovascular Risk](/pmid-38446477)

Garcia-Argibay M, et al. JAMA Netw Open. 2024 Mar. PMID: 38446477 · Cohort study

In this cohort study, individuals receiving methylphenidate had a small increased cardiovascular risk vs matched controls in the 6 months after treatment initiation. However, there was little evidence for an increased risk of 20% or higher and for differences in risk increase between people with and without a history of cardiovascular disease. Therefore, before treatment initiation, careful consideration of the risk-benefit trade-off of methylphenidate would be useful, regardless of cardiovascular history.

#### [Adult Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder and the Risk of Dementia](/pmid-37847497)

Levine SZ, et al. JAMA Netw Open. 2023 Oct. PMID: 37847497 · Cohort study

In this cohort study of individuals born between 1933 and 1952 and followed up in old age, adult ADHD was associated with an increased risk of dementia. Policy makers, caregivers, patients, and clinicians may wish to monitor reliably for ADHD in old age.

#### [In utero exposure to ADHD medication and long-term offspring outcomes](/pmid-36759544)

#### [Association of Perinatal and Childhood Ischemic Stroke With Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder](/pmid-35471571)

Bolk J, et al. JAMA Netw Open. 2022 Apr. PMID: 35471571 · Cohort study

This cohort study of 1320 children with pediatric ischemic stroke suggests that there is an increased risk of ADHD, particularly in children with adverse motor outcomes and/or epilepsy, compared with controls. The risk increases after childhood strokes regardless of comorbidities.

#### [Insomnia in Tourette Syndrome and Chronic Tic Disorder](/pmid-34693569)

Isomura K, et al. Mov Disord. 2022 Feb. PMID: 34693569 · Cohort study

Insomnia is significantly associated with TS/CTD, independently from somatic disorders, familial factors or psychiatric comorbidities, although familial factors, neurodevelopmental comorbidities, and ADHD/ADHD medication may explain part of the association. Insomnia should be routinely assessed and managed in TS/CTD, particularly in chronic patients and in those with comorbid ADHD. Other sleep disorders require further study. © 2021 The Authors. #### [ADHD medication discontinuation and persistence across the lifespan: a retrospective observational study using population-based databases](/pmid-38035876)

Brikell I, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2024 Jan. PMID: 38035876

Early medication discontinuation is prevalent in ADHD treatment, particularly among young adults. Although reinitiation of medication is common, treatment persistence in adolescents and young adults is lower than expected based on previous estimates of ADHD symptom persistence in these age groups. This study highlights the scope of medication treatment discontinuation and persistence in ADHD across the lifespan and provides new knowledge about long-term ADHD medication use.
