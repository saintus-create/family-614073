## Evidence synthesis

#### [ADHD and adherence to antihypertensive medication treatment: a multinational cohort study](/pmid-41721349)

Yao H, et al. BMC Med. 2026 Feb. PMID: 41721349 · Meta-analysis

Adults with ADHD are more likely to discontinue antihypertensive treatment and exhibit poor medication adherence. However, ADHD medication use appears to be associated with better adherence among individuals with ADHD.

#### [Psychiatric and neurological predictors of early ADHD medication discontinuation across the lifespan: a multinational study](/pmid-42392681)

Brikell I, et al. BMJ Ment Health. 2026 Jul. PMID: 42392681 · Meta-analysis

Children with neuropsychiatric comorbidity and related comedication are more likely to discontinue ADHD medication early, whereas few consistent predictors were seen from adolescence onwards. Marked cross-country variation, particularly in the USA, points to system-level influences on treatment patterns.

#### [Long term methylphenidate exposure and growth in children and adolescents with ADHD. A systematic review and meta-analysis](/pmid-33080250)

Carucci S, et al. Neurosci Biobehav Rev. 2021 Jan. PMID: 33080250 · Meta-analysis

Long-term treatment with MPH can result in reduction in height and weight. However, effect sizes are small with possible minimal clinical impact. Long-term prospective studies may help to clarify the underlying biological drivers and specific mediators and moderators.

#### [Medication adherence and persistence in children and adolescents with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD): a systematic review and qualitative update](/pmid-39105823)

Ferrin M, et al. Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry. 2025 Mar. PMID: 39105823 · Systematic review

Our findings indicate that medication-adherence and persistence among youth with ADHD are generally poor and have not changed in recent years. Clinicians need to be aware that various factors may contribute to poor adherence/persistence and that long-acting stimulants and psychoeducational programs may help to improve adherence/persistence.

#### [The future of child and adolescent clinical psychopharmacology: A systematic review of phase 2, 3, or 4 randomized controlled trials of pharmacologic agents without regulatory approval or for unapproved indications](/pmid-37001575)

Cortese S, et al. Neurosci Biobehav Rev. 2023 Jun. PMID: 37001575 · Systematic review

#### [Treatment with methylphenidate and the risk of fractures among children and young people: A systematic review and self-controlled case series study](/pmid-36918367)

Gao L, et al. Br J Clin Pharmacol. 2023 Aug. PMID: 36918367 · Systematic review

Methylphenidate treatment may lower the risk of all-cause fractures from both study designs; however, further evidence is needed about the treatment duration and sex effect. Conclusions on stress fractures are not yet established, and further research is required.

#### [Safety, Tolerability and Efficacy of Drugs for Treating Behavioural Insomnia in Children with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Systematic Review with Methodological Quality Assessment](/pmid-28391425)

Anand S, et al. Paediatr Drugs. 2017 Jun. PMID: 28391425 · Systematic review

There is generally poor evidence for prescribing drugs for behavioural insomnia in children with ADHD. Further controlled studies are warranted.

## Trials

#### [Long-term safety of methylphenidate in children and adolescents with ADHD: 2-year outcomes of the Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder Drugs Use Chronic Effects (ADDUCE) study](/pmid-36958362)

Man KKC, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2023 May. PMID: 36958362 · Randomized controlled trial

Our results suggest that long-term treatment with methylphenidate for 2 years is safe. There was no evidence to support the hypothesis that methylphenidate treatment leads to reductions in growth. Methylphenidate-related pulse and blood pressure changes, although relatively small, require regular monitoring.

## Observational studies

#### [Family-based association study of DRD4 gene in methylphenidate-responded Attention Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder](/pmid-28282463)

Leung PW, et al. PLoS One. 2017. PMID: 28282463 · Case-control study

#### [Trends in attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder medication use: a retrospective observational study using population-based databases](/pmid-30220514)

Raman SR, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2018 Oct. PMID: 30220514 · Cross-sectional study

Using a common protocol and data from 13 countries and one SAR, these results show increases over time but large variations in ADHD medication use in multiple regions. The recommendations of evidence-based guidelines need to be followed consistently in clinical practice. Further research is warranted to describe the safety and effectiveness of ADHD medication in the short and long term, and to inform evidence-based guidelines, particularly in adults.

#### [ADHD Drug Prescribing Trend Is Increasing Among Children and Adolescents in Hong Kong](/pmid-24994875)

Man KKC, et al. J Atten Disord. 2017 Dec. PMID: 24994875 · Cross-sectional study

The prevalence of ADHD medication prescribing in Hong Kong is increasing but remains lower than most Western countries. However, the prevalence of ADHD medication prescribing for kindergarten children should be monitored to ensure appropriate use.

## Reviews

#### [Neurological and psychiatric adverse effects of long-term methylphenidate treatment in ADHD: A map of the current evidence](/pmid-31545988)

Krinzinger H, et al. Neurosci Biobehav Rev. 2019 Dec. PMID: 31545988 · Review

Although some evidence suggests an elevated risk of psychosis and tics, case reports describe remission on discontinuation. Several studies suggest that long-term MPH may reduce depression and suicide in ADHD.

#### [Lost in transition: A review of the unmet need of patients with attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder transitioning to adulthood](/pmid-27552649)

Treuer T, et al. Asia Pac Psychiatry. 2017 Jun. PMID: 27552649 · Review

The review suggests that the transition period to adult ADHD may be an underresearched and underserved area. The transition period plays an important role regarding how ADHD symptoms may be perceived and acted upon by adult psychiatrists.

## Other records

#### [ADHD medication discontinuation and persistence across the lifespan: a retrospective observational study using population-based databases](/pmid-38035876)

Brikell I, et al. Lancet Psychiatry. 2024 Jan. PMID: 38035876

Early medication discontinuation is prevalent in ADHD treatment, particularly among young adults. Although reinitiation of medication is common, treatment persistence in adolescents and young adults is lower than expected based on previous estimates of ADHD symptom persistence in these age groups. This study highlights the scope of medication treatment discontinuation and persistence in ADHD across the lifespan and provides new knowledge about long-term ADHD medication use.

#### [Descriptive analysis of poisoning cases involving attention deficit hyperactivity disorder medications in Hong Kong](/pmid-37303104)

Gao L, et al. Hong Kong Med J. 2023 Jun. PMID: 37303104

No significant correlation was evident between ADHD medication prescriptions and poisoning events involving ADHD medications. However, medication management and caregiver education must be emphasised to prevent potential poisoning events.

#### [Multifamily Therapy for Children With ADHD in Hong Kong: The Different Impacts on Fathers and Mothers](/pmid-29405810)

Lai KYC, et al. J Atten Disord. 2021 Jan. PMID: 29405810

#### [Treatment with Methylphenidate for Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and the Risk of All-Cause Poisoning in Children and Adolescents: A Self-Controlled Case Series Study](/pmid-34283391)

Gao L, et al. CNS Drugs. 2021 Jul. PMID: 34283391

The risk of all-cause poisoning was higher shortly before and after the first MPH prescription and became lower during the subsequent prescription period. Our results do not support an association between the use of MPH and an increased risk of all-cause poisoning in children and adolescents and, in fact, suggest that longer-term use of MPH may be associated with a lower risk of all-cause poisoning, although this latter finding requires further study.

#### [Association between methylphenidate treatment and risk of seizure: a population-based, self-controlled case-series study](/pmid-32450123)

Man KKC, et al. Lancet Child Adolesc Health. 2020 Jun. PMID: 32450123

The incidence of seizures was higher in the period immediately after the start of methylphenidate treatment than in the non-exposed period. No increased risk was observed during continuation of methylphenidate treatment. The association between methylphenidate treatment and seizures immediately after initiation of medication can be seen as a potential safety signal. Monitoring of neurological outcomes in individuals with ADHD is recommended when they first start methylphenidate treatment.

#### [Association of Risk of Suicide Attempts With Methylphenidate Treatment](/pmid-28746699)

Man KKC, et al. JAMA Psychiatry. 2017 Oct. PMID: 28746699

The incidence of suicide attempts was higher in the period immediately before the start of methylphenidate treatment. The risk remained elevated immediately after the start of methylphenidate treatment and returned to baseline levels during continuation of methylphenidate treatment. The observed higher risk of suicide attempts before treatment may reflect emerging psychiatric symptoms that trigger medical consultations that result in a decision to begin ADHD treatment. Therefore, this study's results do not support a causal association between methylphenidate treatment and suicide attempts.
