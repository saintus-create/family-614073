## Evidence is an input, not a magic authorization

The evidence module should answer two separate questions:

1. What evidence exists for the proposed use?
2. How does that evidence apply to this patient and clinical decision?

A paper should never be treated as automatically establishing standard of care merely because it contains a positive result.

## Evidence record

For each source, capture:

- Citation and persistent identifier.
- Study design.
- Population and inclusion criteria.
- Intervention, dose, and formulation.
- Comparator.
- Outcomes.
- Effect estimates and uncertainty where reported.
- Adverse events and discontinuations.
- Duration and follow-up.
- Limitations and risk of bias.
- Relevance to the proposed patient.
- Whether the source supports, weakens, or is neutral toward the proposed use.

## Contradictory evidence

A defensible dossier must search for evidence that cuts against the proposed use. Record meaningful negative findings, safety signals, failed trials, guideline disagreement, and limitations rather than selectively collecting favorable studies.

## Evidence-to-rationale bridge

The final record should explicitly connect the literature to the patient:

`Evidence → clinical inference → patient-specific facts → treatment rationale → monitoring plan`

This bridge is the part that a bibliography cannot provide.
