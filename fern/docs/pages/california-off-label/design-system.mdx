<Callout intent="info" title="Design principle" icon="fa-regular fa-compass">
The interface should feel like a clinical research workspace, not a generic documentation template. Icons communicate semantic meaning; color reinforces meaning; typography, spacing, and restrained graphic motifs establish hierarchy.
</Callout>

## The visual grammar

The application uses four semantic families. Each family should remain visually distinct and should not be mixed merely for decoration.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Authority" icon="fa-regular fa-landmark">
    Statutes, regulations, agency materials, FDA labeling, and provenance. Use institutional and documentary symbols.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Clinical" icon="fa-regular fa-stethoscope">
    Patient evaluation, indication, consent, prescribing, and monitoring. Use medical and human-facing symbols.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Evidence" icon="fa-regular fa-flask">
    Studies, trials, guidelines, safety findings, and comparative analysis. Use scientific and analytical symbols.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Decision" icon="fa-regular fa-gavel">
    Coverage, authorization, denial, appeal, modification, and outcome. Use adjudication and workflow symbols.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Graphic language

Graphics are identifiers, not illustrations. They should help answer **where am I, what kind of information is this, and what happens next?**

<Steps>
  <Step title="Authority thread">
    Use thin rules, documentary geometry, and institutional marks for legal and regulatory material. Avoid ornamental courthouse imagery.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evidence thread">
    Use connected nodes, restrained scientific motifs, and comparison geometry to show relationships among studies, findings, limitations, and conclusions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Workflow thread">
    Use a continuous path through Define → Identify → Evaluate → Discuss → Document → Monitor → Authorize. The path should feel like one process, not seven unrelated pages.
  </Step>
  <Step title="California signal">
    California can appear through restrained jurisdictional markers, sectional labels, and subtle line geometry. Avoid turning the product into a government-portal imitation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout intent="tip" title="Beautiful means useful here" icon="fa-regular fa-sparkles">
A visual element earns its place when it improves recognition, orientation, comparison, or hierarchy. If it merely fills empty space, remove it.
</Callout>

<Callout intent="warning" title="Avoid icon drift">
Do not introduce a new icon when a canonical semantic icon already exists. A recognizable icon system depends on repetition. Decorative icons should not compete with workflow or authority indicators.
</Callout>

## Navigation behavior

The sidebar is a wayfinding system, not a miniature database index.

- Keep labels concise enough to recognize at a glance.
- Truncate long labels with an ellipsis rather than wrapping them into several lines.
- Keep the complete title on the destination page.
- Use section labels and icons to provide context instead of embedding context in every page title.
- Collapse secondary collections and deep research categories by default.
- Keep the provider workflow visible and short.
- Prefer `Authority`, `Evidence`, `Consent`, `Documentation`, `Coverage`, and `Decisions` over paragraph-length menu labels.

This creates progressive disclosure: the sidebar tells the provider where to go; the page tells the provider what they need to know.

## Status language

<Badge intent="check">Verified</Badge> means the source or proposition has been checked against its designated primary source.

<Badge intent="info" minimal>Advisory</Badge> identifies guidance that informs practice without being presented as a mandatory statutory requirement.

<Badge intent="warning" minimal>Review required</Badge> identifies a proposition whose applicability, currency, or source hierarchy requires provider review.

<Badge intent="error" minimal>Not established</Badge> means the application does not have sufficient evidence to assert the proposition as a requirement.

## Interaction principles

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Progressive disclosure">
    Show the provider the next decision that matters. Keep secondary legal detail available through linked authority records, accordions, and tooltips rather than forcing every provider through a wall of text.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Provenance">
    Every legal or evidentiary assertion should be traceable to a source. Use source links, verification metadata, and explicit authority classifications rather than anonymous citations.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Comparison">
    When presenting claims or appeal decisions, show the factual pattern, evidence, governing standard, decision, and appeal history side by side. Never reduce a complex decision to a green check or red X alone.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Accessibility">
    Icons never carry meaning alone. Pair them with text, labels, or accessible names. Color is supplemental, not the sole indicator of status. Respect reduced-motion preferences for animated background treatments.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Design-system rule

The site should look increasingly coherent as content grows. New statutes, forms, studies, and decisions should inherit the same semantic vocabulary rather than introducing a new visual treatment for every content type.
