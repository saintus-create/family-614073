# Provider workflow

This is the eventual UI workflow. The provider should not have to know which statute answers which question before starting.

## Stage 1 — Define

**Drug** → **formulation** → **patient** → **condition** → **proposed indication** → **dose/regimen**

The system determines what is FDA-approved and identifies that the proposed use is on-label or off-label.

## Stage 2 — Identify applicable law

The system evaluates:

- California prescribing authority.
- Controlled-substance status.
- Telehealth status.
- Patient population.
- Practice setting.
- Payer/program.
- Workers' compensation status.
- Drug- or indication-specific statutes and regulations.

## Stage 3 — Build evidence dossier

The provider reviews ranked evidence and can pin supporting and contradictory sources. The system should show study design and applicability rather than merely a citation count.

## Stage 4 — Clinical rationale

The provider records patient-specific facts and explains why the proposed treatment is medically appropriate, including alternatives and expected benefits/risks.

## Stage 5 — Consent

The system identifies applicable disclosure items and generates a documentation checklist. It should never silently convert a recommended disclosure into a claim that California law requires it.

## Stage 6 — Prescribing record

Generate a structured record containing the clinical rationale, evidence, consent documentation, monitoring plan, and authority provenance.

## Stage 7 — Coverage

If the provider needs payer authorization, transfer the relevant clinical evidence and rationale into the existing coverage workflow. Do not duplicate the clinical record unnecessarily.

## Stage 8 — Follow-up

Record response, adverse effects, monitoring results, continuation rationale, dose changes, and discontinuation decisions.

## UI principles

The interface should expose complexity progressively:

**Provider-facing:** one workflow, clear decisions, minimal legal jargon.

**Evidence-facing:** expandable source cards, study metadata, contradictory evidence, and provenance.

**Legal-facing:** exact citation, quoted operative language, applicability, effective date, and primary source.

**Audit-facing:** immutable source/version timestamps and the reasoning path used to produce the final record.
