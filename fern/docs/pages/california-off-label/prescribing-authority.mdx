## Core authorities

### Business and Professions Code § 2242

Establish the patient's prior examination and the medical indication for the prescription of a dangerous drug, subject to the statute's requirements and exceptions. Telehealth does not eliminate the applicable standard of care.

[California Legislative Information: BPC § 2242](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=2242.)

### Business and Professions Code § 2234

Review the Medical Board's disciplinary authority and standards concerning unprofessional conduct, including gross negligence, repeated negligent acts, and incompetence.

[California Legislative Information: BPC § 2234](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=2234)

### Health and Safety Code § 11153

For controlled substances, evaluate the requirement that the prescription be issued for a legitimate medical purpose and in the usual course of professional practice.

[California Legislative Information: HSC § 11153](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=11153)

### Business and Professions Code § 2290.5

When telehealth is used, evaluate the statutory disclosure, consent, and documentation requirements.

[California Legislative Information: BPC § 2290.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=2290.5)

## Provider checklist

- Confirm licensure and prescribing authority.
- Document the patient evaluation required for the proposed treatment.
- Document the medical indication.
- Identify whether the drug is controlled.
- Identify whether telehealth requirements apply.
- Identify any setting-specific requirements.
- Record the patient-specific clinical reasoning.
- Preserve supporting evidence and the date it was reviewed.

## What this page does not establish

The existence of an FDA-approved drug does not by itself establish that every off-label use satisfies California's standard of care. Conversely, the fact that a use is off-label does not by itself establish that the use is prohibited. The clinical and legal analysis must be performed at the level of the particular prescription.
