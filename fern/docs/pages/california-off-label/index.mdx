## The prescribing pathway

<Steps toc={true} tocDepth={2}>

<Step title="Identify the proposed use">
Record the drug, formulation, dose, route, patient population, diagnosis, and proposed indication. Compare the proposed use against current FDA-approved labeling.

[FDA labeling and regulatory archive](/regulatory)
</Step>

<Step title="Establish authority and clinical indication">
Document the applicable California authority, patient evaluation, diagnosis or condition, and why treatment is medically indicated for this particular patient.

[California authority registry](/california-off-label/authority-registry)
</Step>

<Step title="Evaluate the evidence">
Identify evidence supporting efficacy and safety, assess quality and relevance, and record material contradictory evidence. An evidence citation is not a substitute for patient-specific clinical reasoning.

[Evidence framework](/california-off-label/evidence)
</Step>

<Step title="Address informed discussion and consent">
Determine what disclosure is required or clinically appropriate for the patient and setting. Separate general informed-consent principles from drug-specific, population-specific, facility-specific, and statutory requirements.

[Consent framework](/california-off-label/consent)
</Step>

<Step title="Complete the prescribing record">
Capture the indication, rationale, evidence, alternatives, risks, benefits, monitoring plan, and follow-up so another qualified reviewer could reconstruct the clinical reasoning.

[Documentation framework](/california-off-label/documentation)
</Step>

<Step title="Check additional regulatory layers">
Determine whether controlled-substance, telehealth, workers' compensation, facility, public-program, or payer rules add requirements.

[Additional requirements](/california-off-label/additional-requirements)
</Step>

<Step title="Separate prescribing from coverage">
A clinically defensible prescription and a payer's obligation to cover it are different legal questions. If coverage is at issue, move into the existing authorization workflow rather than conflating the two.

[Coverage authorization](/coverage)
</Step>

</Steps>

## System architecture

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Authority">
    **Question:** What law or regulation applies to these facts?

    **Output:** Authority checklist with citation, trigger, obligation type, effective date, and provenance.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Clinical reasoning">
    **Question:** Why is this proposed use appropriate for this patient?

    **Output:** Patient-specific indication and rationale, alternatives, expected benefits, material risks, and monitoring plan.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Evidence">
    **Question:** What supports or limits the proposed use?

    **Output:** Evidence dossier containing study design, relevance, supporting findings, limitations, and contradictory evidence.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Coverage">
    **Question:** Will a payer or program authorize or cover it?

    **Output:** Separate authorization or appeal packet using the applicable coverage standard.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Trust and provenance

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Primary authority" icon="fa-regular fa-landmark">
    Statutes and regulations are distinguished from agency guidance, clinical guidelines, and peer-reviewed evidence.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Evidence provenance" icon="fa-regular fa-flask">
    Studies and guidelines retain source, design, relevance, limitations, and verification metadata.

  <Card title="Decision context" icon="fa-regular fa-gavel">
    Authorization outcomes remain tied to their facts, governing standard, evidence, and appeal history.

<Callout intent="tip" title="Interface language" icon="fa-regular fa-circle-info">
The visual system deliberately distinguishes authority, clinical, evidence, and decision concepts. See the [interface and iconography system](/california-off-label/design-system) for the canonical vocabulary.
</Callout>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Mandatory is not the same as advisory">
    A statute or regulation may impose an obligation. Agency guidance may explain how an agency expects a rule to operate. A guideline may inform clinical reasoning. The application should preserve those distinctions instead of flattening every source into a generic “requirement.”
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Off-label does not mean unlawful">
    Off-label status is a classification that triggers evidence, clinical-reasoning, consent, documentation, and applicability questions. It is not by itself a universal prohibition or permission.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Approved and denied claims are decision evidence">
    Real-world authorization outcomes will be catalogued with their fact pattern, evidence, cited authority, decision rationale, and appeal history. They are not substitutes for governing law.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Callout intent="warning" title="Use primary authority before relying" icon="fa-regular fa-triangle-exclamation">
This resource is a research and documentation tool, not legal or medical advice. Requirements can vary by prescriber type, patient, drug, clinical setting, payer, and applicable program. Verify primary authority before relying on any proposition.
</Callout>
