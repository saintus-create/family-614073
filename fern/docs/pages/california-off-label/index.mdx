## What this system does

California does not reduce off-label prescribing to a single permission form. The defensible prescribing record is assembled from several distinct questions: authority to prescribe, patient-specific medical indication, applicable standard of care, evidence supporting the proposed use, informed discussion and consent where appropriate, documentation, monitoring, and any additional requirements imposed by controlled-substance law, telehealth, the treatment setting, or the payer.

This section keeps those layers separate so a provider can see exactly which authority supports which conclusion.

## The prescribing pathway

<Steps toc={true}>

<Step title="Identify the proposed use">

Record the drug, formulation, dose, route, patient population, diagnosis, and proposed indication. Compare the proposed use against the current FDA-approved labeling.

[FDA labeling and regulatory archive](/regulatory)

</Step>

<Step title="Establish California prescribing authority and clinical indication">

Document the applicable California authority, the patient evaluation, the diagnosis or condition being treated, and why treatment is medically indicated for this particular patient.

[California prescribing authority](/california-off-label/prescribing-authority)

</Step>

<Step title="Evaluate the evidence">

Identify evidence supporting efficacy and safety, assess its quality and relevance, and record material contradictory evidence. Do not treat an evidence citation as a substitute for patient-specific clinical reasoning.

[Evidence framework](/california-off-label/evidence)

</Step>

<Step title="Address informed discussion and consent">

Determine what disclosure is required or clinically appropriate for the patient and setting. Separate general informed-consent principles from drug-specific, population-specific, facility-specific, and statutory requirements.

[Consent framework](/california-off-label/consent)

</Step>

<Step title="Complete the prescribing record">

Capture the indication, rationale, evidence, alternatives, risks, benefits, monitoring plan, and follow-up. The objective is that another qualified reviewer could reconstruct the clinical reasoning from the record.

[Documentation framework](/california-off-label/documentation)

</Step>

<Step title="Check additional regulatory layers">

Determine whether controlled-substance, telehealth, workers' compensation, facility, public-program, or payer rules add requirements.

[Additional requirements](/california-off-label/additional-requirements)

</Step>

<Step title="Separate prescribing from coverage">

A clinically defensible prescription and a payer's obligation to cover it are different legal questions. If coverage is at issue, move into the existing authorization workflow rather than conflating the two.

[Coverage authorization](/coverage)

</Step>

</Steps>

## System architecture

| Layer | Core question | Primary output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FDA | What is approved and labeled? | Label comparison |
| California law | May the practitioner prescribe and under what conditions? | Authority checklist |
| Clinical reasoning | Why is this use appropriate for this patient? | Patient-specific rationale |
| Evidence | What supports efficacy and safety? | Evidence dossier |
| Consent | What must or should be disclosed? | Consent record |
| Documentation | Can the decision be reconstructed? | Prescribing record |
| Monitoring | How will benefit and harm be assessed? | Follow-up plan |
| Coverage | Will a payer authorize or cover it? | Authorization packet |

## Build principle

Every proposition in the system should eventually carry structured metadata: authority type, citation, jurisdiction, effective date, applicability conditions, mandatory/advisory status, source URL, and verification date.

That makes the system auditable instead of turning it into another enormous pile of vaguely authoritative-looking hyperlinks, a genre humanity has already perfected.
