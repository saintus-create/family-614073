## Why this layer exists

Off-label status is only one part of the analysis. Additional law may apply because of the drug, the patient, the practice setting, the delivery method, or the payment system.

## Controlled substances

Determine whether the drug is controlled and apply the California and federal controlled-substance requirements that correspond to the prescription.

[Health & Safety Code § 11153](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=HSC&sectionNum=11153)

## Telehealth

If care is delivered by telehealth, evaluate the requirements governing disclosure, consent, standard of care, and documentation.

[Business and Professions Code § 2290.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=2290.5)

## Workers' compensation

Workers' compensation treatment has its own utilization-review and Medical Treatment Utilization Schedule framework. Do not automatically apply commercial health-plan coverage rules to a workers' compensation prescription.

[California Division of Workers' Compensation](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dwc/)

## Payer coverage

Coverage and prescribing are distinct. When authorization is required, use the existing coverage workflow and preserve the provider's clinical rationale and evidence dossier.

[Coverage authorization](/coverage)

## Setting-specific rules

The application should eventually support requirements for hospitals, clinics, behavioral-health facilities, correctional settings, public programs, and other regulated environments as independent applicability modules.

## Applicability engine

Future implementation should ask:

`drug → patient → indication → prescriber → setting → delivery method → payer → program`

and return only the authorities that actually apply.
