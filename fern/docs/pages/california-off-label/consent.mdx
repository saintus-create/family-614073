<Callout intent="warning">
Consent requirements depend on the treatment, patient, setting, and applicable law. This page is a framework for identifying requirements, not a universal consent-form mandate.
</Callout>

## Off-label status

The system should allow the provider to document that the proposed indication differs from the FDA-approved labeling and to record the material information discussed with the patient.

## Discussion record

Capture, as applicable:

- Proposed treatment and intended purpose.
- Whether the proposed use is off-label.
- Expected benefits and uncertainty.
- Material risks and adverse effects.
- Reasonable alternatives, including no treatment.
- Patient-specific factors affecting the risk-benefit assessment.
- Questions raised by the patient.
- Patient decision and any limitations on consent.
- Date, participants, and method of discussion.

## Requirement classification

Each consent item should be tagged as one of:

- Statutorily required.
- Required by regulation.
- Required by the applicable clinical setting or policy.
- Professionally appropriate documentation.
- Patient-specific material information.

Do not label a best practice as a statutory command. That distinction is legally important and somehow still routinely lost in healthcare websites.

## Special requirements

The application should support drug-specific, population-specific, facility-specific, and telehealth-specific consent rules as separate records rather than embedding them into one universal template.
