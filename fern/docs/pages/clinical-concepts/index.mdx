The Clinical Concept Index sits above the document catalogue. It recognizes the clinical idea in a question, expands its terminology, maps it to policies and requirements, and then retrieves evidence according to source authority.

This prevents a literal keyword search from becoming a false answer. A question does not need to use the exact title of a policy to be answerable; the index should be able to assemble a scoped answer from related, authoritative sources.

## Evidence architecture

```text
Question
  ↓
Clinical concept recognition
  ↓
Concept expansion and synonyms
  ↓
Policy + regulation + criteria mapping
  ↓
Evidence retrieval
  ↓
Authority ranking
  ↓
Answer synthesis
  ↓
Citation + confidence
  ↓
Gap detection
```

## Authority hierarchy

When sources disagree or answer different parts of a question, the index preserves the distinction rather than flattening everything into one result.

1. Federal law and regulation
2. California law and regulation
3. DHCS Medi-Cal requirements
4. CenCal contract requirements
5. CenCal policy
6. CenCal clinical criteria
7. CenCal provider manual
8. CenCal operational guidance
9. Clinical guideline
10. Educational or member material

The hierarchy is a ranking aid, not a substitute for checking the source's scope, effective date, benefit channel, and verification state.

## Benchmark concept

[Polypharmacy](/clinical-concepts/polypharmacy) is the first benchmark concept. It tests whether the index can answer a clinically obvious question even when the exact term is absent from the plan's published documents.

## Plan obligation dimension

[Plan Obligations & Member/Provider Requests](/clinical-concepts/plan-obligations) adds trigger, obligation, standard, procedure, deadline, responsible entity, and escalation path to the concept record. [Previously Authorized Treatment](/clinical-concepts/previously-authorized) is the first continuity-of-care benchmark for this dimension.

## Responsibility resolver

[Jurisdiction & Responsibility Map](/clinical-concepts/jurisdiction-responsibility) resolves who administers the benefit, who operates the plan, which department owns the issue, what authority governs it, how to reach the operator, and where to escalate when the responsibility is not fulfilled.

The [COHS Plan Map](/cohs-map) provides the visual entry point for plan identity and service-area orientation, with plan-logo markers and links into the responsibility resolver.

## Coverage decision and consistency layer

[Coverage Decision Anatomy & Prior Decision History](/clinical-concepts/coverage-decision-anatomy) separates formulary status, prior authorization, off-label use, quantity and days' supply, medical necessity, request type, decision-maker, and longitudinal changes. Its Decision Consistency layer asks what changed when a long-approved therapy later becomes repeatedly denied.

## Concept record contract

Every canonical concept record should provide a definition, synonyms, related concepts, relevant services or medications, mapped CenCal policies, mapped DHCS requirements, applicable clinical guidance, member and provider resources, a review date, and a source-authority classification.

## Index quality signals

- **Direct policy found:** the corpus contains a source explicitly addressing the concept.
- **Related policies found:** the corpus contains authoritative sources that address the concept through adjacent functions.
- **Regulatory authority found:** a governing requirement supports the mapped function.
- **Confidence:** a scoped synthesis of authority, coverage, recency, and directness.
- **Concept gap:** retrieval found material, but not a coherent or sufficiently authoritative answer.

A concept gap is an index maintenance signal. It should trigger creation or refresh of the concept record instead of silently returning the highest-scoring document.

## Boundary

This index is a research and navigation layer. It does not prescribe treatment, determine an individual member's coverage, replace clinical judgment, or make an authorization decision. Current plan documents, criteria, forms, and effective dates require verification before operational use.
