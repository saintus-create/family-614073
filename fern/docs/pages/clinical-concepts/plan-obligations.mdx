## Purpose

The Clinical Concept Index now includes a **Plan Obligations & Member/Provider Requests** dimension. It answers a different question from document search:

> What is the responsible entity required to do when this member or provider request occurs?

This layer distinguishes a plan's discretion from an affirmative obligation, and it preserves the operational sequence and deadline that can be lost when requirements are represented only as document chunks.

## Requirement record

Every indexed requirement should capture five fields:

| Field | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | What happened? | A provider requests continuation of an existing treatment. |
| Obligation | What must the responsible entity do? | Review the request under applicable continuity-of-care and authorization requirements. |
| Standard | What governs the decision? | Medical necessity, DHCS requirements, continuity-of-care rules, and clinical criteria. |
| Procedure | What must happen operationally? | Receive → document → review → determine → notify → arrange service or provide appeal rights. |
| Deadline | How quickly must it happen? | Capture the source-specific timeframe; urgent conditions may require faster action. |

A record is incomplete if it says what may happen but omits who owns the benefit, who has clinical responsibility, or what response deadline applies.

## Responsibility graph

```text
Request
  ↓
Benefit category
  ↓
Responsible entity
  ↓
Applicable authority
  ↓
Plan responsibility + member/provider responsibility
  ↓
Required action
  ↓
Deadline
  ↓
Escalation path
```

This prevents a technically correct but operationally incomplete answer such as “Here is a pharmacy policy” when the real question is “Who must prevent interruption of an already established treatment, and what must happen after the provider asks?”

## Benefit ownership is not clinical responsibility

Medi-Cal pharmacy coverage is not one monolithic bucket. Medi-Cal Rx administers prescription drug benefits, while managed-care plans cover certain provider-administered drugs. The index must therefore route the request to the correct benefit owner before describing the plan's obligations.

| Request type | First routing question | Verification boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New prescription drug request | Is this a Medi-Cal Rx pharmacy benefit? | Confirm current Medi-Cal Rx routing and criteria. |
| Provider-administered drug | Is this a medical or institutional claim? | Confirm CenCal medical-pharmacy responsibility and any carve-out. |
| Existing treatment continuation | Is there an active authorization or continuity-of-care fact? | Confirm authorization duration, transition rules, and applicable notice. |
| Urgent medication request | Does delay risk the member's condition? | Confirm expedited review standard and deadline. |
| Administrative interruption | Was treatment interrupted without a new clinical decision? | Escalate for continuity, notice, and remediation analysis. |

## Query format

“What is the plan required to do?” should be a primary query mode of the COHS site, alongside evidence search and workflow navigation. A useful answer should return the trigger, responsible entity, authority, obligation, procedure, deadline, and escalation path with citations and confidence.

Before answering, route the request through the [Jurisdiction & Responsibility Map](/clinical-concepts/jurisdiction-responsibility) so the response identifies the benefit administrator, plan operator, responsible department, and escalation authority rather than assuming they are the same organization.

## Safety and verification boundary

This index is a structured research aid. It does not determine an individual member's eligibility, coverage, medical necessity, or appeal rights. Confirm the current DHCS requirement, plan contract, benefit channel, effective date, source-specific deadline, and responsible entity before operational use.
