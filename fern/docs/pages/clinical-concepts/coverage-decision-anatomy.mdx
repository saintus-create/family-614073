## The real coverage question

“Is this drug covered?” is often too coarse to explain a Medi-Cal decision. The index must distinguish **not on the formulary**, **subject to prior authorization**, **restricted by utilization limits**, **not medically necessary under the applicable standard**, and **categorically excluded or prohibited**. These are different findings with different evidence and appeal implications.

An off-label request is not automatically a non-covered request. Supporting literature, compendia, or treatment guidelines may be relevant to an off-label prior-authorization review. Quantity and days' supply are separate clinical and utilization variables, not merely clerical details.

## Coverage Decision Anatomy

Every medication request should be decomposed into the following record:

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Drug | What product, formulation, and route are requested? |
| Coverage status | Is it covered, non-covered, restricted, or subject to a carve-out? |
| FDA indication | What indication is approved for the requested product? |
| Requested indication | What condition is the prescriber treating? |
| Off-label | Is the requested use outside the FDA labeling? |
| Formulary status | Is the product on the applicable list, and what restrictions apply? |
| PA requirement | Is prior authorization required even if the product is listed? |
| Quantity and days' supply | Is the issue a utilization limit rather than the drug itself? |
| Dosing | What frequency, dose, and duration are requested? |
| Step therapy | Are alternatives required or relevant? |
| Clinical criteria | Which criteria are being applied? |
| Medical necessity | What standard governs the individualized determination? |
| Documentation | What clinical records and evidence were submitted? |
| Alternatives | What therapies were tried, failed, contraindicated, or not tolerated? |
| History | Was there a prior authorization, denial, appeal, or expiration? |
| Decision path | Which entity decided, under what deadline, and how can it be escalated? |

## Useful answer patterns

The index should be able to say:

- **Covered product, authorization required:** The drug is a covered product, but the requested indication or utilization pattern requires prior authorization.
- **Off-label is not automatically prohibited:** The requested indication is off-label, but the mapped policy does not establish an absolute exclusion; the relevant question is whether individualized medical-necessity criteria are satisfied.
- **Quantity-based issue:** The requested dosing exceeds an ordinary utilization limit, so the issue is quantity or days-supply authorization rather than categorical formulary exclusion.
- **Administrative versus clinical denial:** The file shows an incomplete request, expired authorization, or routing issue rather than a clinical finding about the drug itself.

These are structured outcomes, not conclusions to be inferred without the current source, benefit channel, and decision record.

## Prior Decision / Treatment History

The index distinguishes:

1. New request
2. Renewal of a previously approved authorization
3. Continuation of previously authorized therapy
4. Repeatedly denied request
5. Previously approved request subsequently denied
6. Long-standing treatment with a new administrator or plan

A renewal is not the same event as an appeal. A renewal follows a previously approved authorization; an appeal addresses a previously denied request. The responsible decision-maker and required documentation may differ.

## Longitudinal decision timeline

A ten-year history should be represented as a timeline rather than a stack of letters:

| Date | Request type | Decision | Entity | Rule/criteria | Reason | What changed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2017 | New request | Approved | Verify benefit owner | Criteria version | Clinical basis | Baseline |
| 2018 | Renewal | Approved | Verify benefit owner | Criteria version | Renewal satisfied | Compare with 2017 |
| 2019 | Renewal | Approved | Verify benefit owner | Criteria version | Renewal satisfied | Compare with 2018 |
| 2020–2026 | Renewal or continuation | Denied | Verify decision-maker | Criteria/policy version | Classify clinical, quantity, administrative, or routing reason | Identify first material change |

## Decision Consistency layer

When the index finds approvals followed by repeated denials, it asks **what changed?** It compares:

- Clinical facts and diagnosis
- Requested product, indication, dose, quantity, and days' supply
- Documentation and supporting literature
- Alternative therapies, contraindications, and intolerances
- Criteria and policy version
- Formulary status and utilization limits
- Benefit administrator and responsible decision-maker
- Prior authorization duration and expiration
- Continuity-of-care or transition facts
- Administrative completeness and submission route

If none of these changed, “denied” is not a complete longitudinal explanation. The index should flag a **decision consistency gap** and request source-level review of the first change point.

## Entity routing

For prescription drugs, route first through the Medi-Cal Rx benefit pathway. For applicable physician-administered drugs billed through the medical or institutional benefit, route through the managed-care plan's medical-pharmacy process. The decision timeline must record the entity that actually had authority at each date; a plan-level source cannot automatically explain a pharmacy-benefit decision.

## Benchmark questions

- Was the requested drug prohibited, or merely subject to prior authorization?
- Was the indication off-label, and what evidence was submitted?
- Was the denial caused by quantity or days' supply?
- Was the PA submitted as a new request, renewal, or appeal?
- Did the prior authorization expire?
- Did the responsible administrator change?
- Did the governing policy or clinical criteria change?
- Was the denial clinical, administrative, or routing-related?
- Did continuity-of-care obligations apply?
- What changed between the last approval and the first denial?

## Verification boundary

This is a decision-reconstruction model, not a coverage determination or appeal outcome. Verify the applicable benefit channel, current Contract Drugs List or plan policy, clinical criteria version, PA history, notice, deadline, decision-maker, and source-effective date before drawing conclusions about a member-specific case.
