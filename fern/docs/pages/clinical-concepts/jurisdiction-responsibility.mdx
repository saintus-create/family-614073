## The first question: who owns this?

The Clinical Concept Index now includes an explicit **Jurisdiction & Responsibility Map**. A useful COHS answer must distinguish the authority that governs a requirement from the entity that administers the benefit, the department that operates the workflow, and the authority that receives an escalation.

> Authority ≠ operator ≠ responsible department ≠ escalation authority.

## Resolver sequence

```text
Member/provider problem or request
  ↓
Who administers the benefit?
  ↓
Who operates the plan?
  ↓
Which department owns the issue?
  ↓
Which authority governs the decision?
  ↓
Who must act and by when?
  ↓
Who can resolve or escalate it?
```

The resolver must not assume that “the county” owns every member issue. A COHS is a county-organized managed-care structure, but the request may be administered by DHCS, Medi-Cal Rx and its contracted administrator, CenCal Health, a CenCal department, a county eligibility interface, or another delivery system.

## Responsibility card

Each resolved issue should produce a structured card:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Issue | The member/provider problem or request. |
| Benefit | Pharmacy, medical, institutional, eligibility, or other category. |
| Primary responsible entity | The organization that owns the transaction or decision. |
| Responsible department | The operational team that receives and acts on the request. |
| Governing authority | DHCS contract, All Plan Letter, statute, regulation, policy, or criteria. |
| Required action | What the responsible entity must do. |
| Time limit | The source-specific deadline, including an expedited standard where applicable. |
| Provider contact | Current number, extension, portal, or submission route. |
| Member contact | Current Member Services route. |
| Escalation | The next responsible authority if the workflow is not fulfilled. |
| Appeal/grievance | The applicable notice, appeal, grievance, or State Fair Hearing mechanism. |
| Sources and last verified | Citations, review state, and verification date. |

## Pharmacy example

A prescription pharmacy benefit should not be labeled simply “CenCal Pharmacy.” The responsibility map distinguishes the prescription pharmacy benefit administered through **Medi-Cal Rx / DHCS and its contracted administrator** from CenCal's **medical-pharmacy management** for applicable physician-administered drugs billed through the medical or institutional benefit.

| Layer | Example responsibility | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory authority | DHCS | Governing program and contract authority |
| Plan operator | CenCal Health | Managed-care plan responsibility where applicable |
| Pharmacy benefit administrator | Medi-Cal Rx / DHCS and contracted administrator | Prescription pharmacy transaction route; verify current administrator |
| Operational department | CenCal Pharmacy Services | Medical-pharmacy management and related utilization workflows where applicable |
| Eligibility interface | County human services department, where applicable | Enrollment or eligibility issue, not automatically clinical authorization |
| Escalation authority | CenCal grievance/appeal pathway, DHCS, or other applicable authority | Depends on benefit, notice, and issue |

## Sample responsibility card: previously authorized medication

| Field | Source-derived working value |
| --- | --- |
| Issue | Previously authorized medication / continuity of care |
| Benefit | Identify prescription pharmacy versus medical benefit first |
| Primary responsible entity | Verification required after benefit routing |
| Responsible department | CenCal Pharmacy Services for applicable medical-pharmacy issues; Medi-Cal Rx route for prescription pharmacy benefit |
| Governing authority | DHCS requirements, applicable plan policy, and benefit-specific criteria |
| Required action | Verify prior authorization and treatment status, identify benefit owner, review continuation or transition requirements, notify member/provider, and arrange service or provide applicable rights |
| Time limit | Source-specific; capture continuity-of-care or expedited-review deadline |
| Provider contact | Verify current provider contact and extension before use |
| Member contact | Verify current Member Services contact before use |
| Escalation | Responsible department → CenCal grievance/appeal pathway → DHCS or other applicable authority |
| Last verified | Verification required |

## Organizational hierarchy

```text
Member / provider
  ↓
CenCal department
  ↓
CenCal Health organizational authority
  ↓
COHS governing structure
  ↓
Santa Barbara or San Luis Obispo County governmental interface, where applicable
  ↓
DHCS division or program
  ↓
California Health & Human Services or other state authority, where applicable
  ↓
CMS or federal authority, where applicable
```

Every relationship in this hierarchy should be labeled and sourced. The hierarchy is a routing model, not an assumption that every issue travels to the top.

## Safety and verification boundary

This map is a responsibility-routing aid. It does not create a legal duty, determine coverage, replace a notice, or guarantee that a contact, vendor, department, or deadline remains current. Confirm the source, effective date, benefit channel, plan contract, current contact route, and applicable member-specific procedure before operational use.
