## Research pathway

<Steps toc={true} tocDepth={2}>
<Step title="Define the question">
State the precise family-law issue and procedural posture before selecting authority. Similar words can conceal materially different legal questions.
</Step>
<Step title="Find governing authority">
Identify the statute, rule, constitutional provision, or controlling case that actually governs the issue. Mark secondary or unverified material as such.
</Step>
<Step title="Test the proposition">
Record the proposition, supporting authority, contrary authority, distinctions, limitations, and effective-date concerns. Do not convert a source's characterization into settled law without review.
</Step>
<Step title="Identify procedure">
Connect the substantive question to filing, notice, service, hearing, evidentiary, review, and appeal requirements that apply to the posture.
</Step>
<Step title="Build the evidence record">
Identify what factual evidence would establish, rebut, or limit each proposition. Keep allegations, testimony, documentary evidence, and judicial findings distinct.
</Step>
<Step title="Select the form">
Use the appropriate current Judicial Council or court form where required, and preserve the relationship between the form, the procedural step, and the authority supporting its use.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Authority graph

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Question">
**Question:** What exactly must be decided?

**Output:** A bounded legal proposition with procedural posture and factual assumptions.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Authority">
**Question:** What source controls or informs the proposition?

**Output:** Authority record with source type, citation, status, effective date, and provenance.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Counterauthority">
**Question:** What limits, distinguishes, contradicts, or modifies the proposition?

**Output:** Counterauthority record linked to the original proposition.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Procedure">
**Question:** What must happen procedurally?

**Output:** Procedural steps, deadlines, notice/service requirements, and applicable forms.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Evidence">
**Question:** What facts establish or rebut the proposition?

**Output:** Evidence map tied to the precise proposition rather than a generalized narrative.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout intent="tip" title="Source status matters" icon="fa-regular fa-circle-info">
A source-derived proposition can be useful research material while remaining unreviewed. Preserve statuses such as verified, source-derived, needs-current-law-review, disputed, superseded, and historical rather than flattening them into one authoritative-looking database.
</Callout>

<Callout intent="warning" title="Verify current authority" icon="fa-regular fa-triangle-exclamation">
Family-law rules can change through legislation, appellate decisions, rule amendments, and local court requirements. Verify current primary authority and applicable local procedures before relying on a proposition or form.
</Callout>
